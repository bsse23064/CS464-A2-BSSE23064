## Four answers

1. **The jump, in numbers.** With `jumpHeight = 1.8 m`, `mass = 1 kg`, and $g = 9.81\text{ m/s}^2$, the launch speed is $v_0 = \sqrt{2 \times 9.81 \times 1.8} = 5.943\text{ m/s}$, giving an impulse `jumpForce` of $m \cdot v_0 = 5.943\text{ N}\cdot\text{s}$. Time to apex is $t_{\text{up}} = v_0 / g = 0.606\text{ s}$, and with `fallMultiplier = 2.2` ($g_{\text{fall}} = 21.582\text{ m/s}^2$), the fall time from 1.8 m is $t_{\text{down}} = \sqrt{2 \times 1.8 / 21.582} = 0.408\text{ s}$, for a total air time of $1.014\text{ s}$. At `speed = 5 m/s`, one jump carries the player $5 \times 1.014 = 5.07\text{ m}$ horizontally on flat ground, which easily clears my widest 3 m gap.

2. **Update and FixedUpdate.** `Update` runs once per rendered frame to poll `Physics.CheckSphere`, read `Horizontal`/`Vertical` axes and `Input.GetKeyDown(KeyCode.Space)`, update the coyote and jump-buffer timestamps, queue the jump, and trigger the landing `Squash()` coroutine. `FixedUpdate` runs on Unity's fixed 0.02 s (50 Hz) physics step to set horizontal `rb.linearVelocity`, fire the queued jump impulse once after zeroing vertical velocity, and apply the `(fallMultiplier - 1f)` downward acceleration. If you swap them, `Input.GetKeyDown` in `FixedUpdate` misses roughly two out of three Space presses at 144 fps because multiple render frames pass between physics steps, while applying impulses and acceleration in `Update` makes jump height and fall speed vary wildly with the frame rate.

3. **Vector3.Dot.** My controller uses `float moveAway = Vector3.Dot(move, flatForward);` inside `Update()` to calculate the scalar projection of the input movement vector against the camera's flattened forward axis (`flatForward`). A positive sign means the player is steering away from the camera (pressing `W`), a negative sign means the player is steering toward the camera (pressing `S`), and a zero means the player is moving purely sideways (`A`/`D`) or standing still.

4. **The juice.** The landing squash uses the `Ease.OutBack` curve over a duration of `0.25 s`, which equals `15 frames` at 60 fps (`0.25 × 60 = 15`). It starts squashed at `y = 0.7` (`x = z = 1 / sqrt(0.7) ≈ 1.195` to preserve volume) on the `Body` child mesh and uses `Mathf.LerpUnclamped` so `OutBack` briefly overshoots past `1.0` before settling at `Vector3.one`. `0.25 s` (15 frames) is long enough for the eye to register the compression and elastic rebound on impact, but short enough that it finishes before the player's next jump or stride.
